02How to choose

How leasing compares to financing a Subaru.

Leasing and financing answer two different questions. Leasing asks: what is the lowest way to drive a new Subaru for a few years? Financing asks: how do I own this vehicle outright over time? Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on how you drive and how long you keep cars.

04An honest fit check

Who a Subaru lease tends to fit.

Leasing is not for everyone, and we will say so plainly. Here is where a lease usually makes sense, and where financing or buying wins instead.

A lease usually fits when:

  • You want predictable payments and a newer vehicle every few years.
  • Your annual mileage fits inside a 10,000 to 12,000 mile cap.
  • You value having the latest safety tech, like updated Subaru EyeSight.
  • You prefer a lower monthly payment over building ownership equity.

Financing or buying usually fits better when:

  • You drive long distances or rack up high annual mileage.
  • You keep vehicles well past the typical three-year lease term.
  • You want to modify or customize your Subaru.
  • You want to own the vehicle free and clear.

If you are not sure which camp you fall into, that is normal. Bring your driving habits and your budget, and we will model both paths side by side through our finance page.

Is leasing cheaper than financing a Subaru?

The monthly payment on a lease is usually lower, but lower payment does not always mean lower total cost. If you drive past the mileage cap or keep cars for many years, financing or buying often costs less overall.

We will model both side by side through our finance page before you decide.

What mileage allowance should I choose on a Subaru lease?

Common lease mileage caps are 10,000 and 12,000 miles per year. The right number depends on how far you actually drive, which we can estimate from your current odometer.

Choosing too low to chase a lower payment often backfires through over-mileage charges at lease end.

Can I lease with a trade-in at Findlay Subaru?

Yes. Trade equity from a current vehicle, including an existing lease, can often roll into a new lease deal.

What happens at the end of a Subaru lease?

You generally have three choices at lease end: return the vehicle, buy it at the agreed residual value, or lease a new Subaru. Mileage and wear are reviewed at return.
